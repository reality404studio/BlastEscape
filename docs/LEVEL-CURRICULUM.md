# Blast Escape — Level Curriculum

This curriculum fixes learning order and approximate pacing. Geometry remains a
separate authoring task governed by `docs/LEVEL-DESIGN-GRAMMAR.md`.

## Pacing target

Twenty-five levels plus the ending should yield a 55–65 minute median first run.
Budget roughly 2–3 minutes per new level including failed attempts, with shorter
introductions and longer synthesis challenges. Measure rather than padding.

## Levels 1–8 — Blast literacy

| Level | Existing intent | Preservation rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | proximity, side choice, first blast launch | broad success window |
| 2 | launch line and air steering through an opening | preserve readable opening |
| 3 | braking and a tighter landing pocket | precision is explicit |
| 4 | first airborne chain | landing between blasts must fail the lesson |
| 5 | hazard/pit synthesis | route stays readable |
| 6 | moving-platform interception | moving target, not blind timing |
| 7 | return arc and recovery hierarchy | locked geometry and route contract |
| 8 | five-blast airborne slalom | all five direction decisions remain load-bearing |

Do not mass-retune these while extracting the core. Treat them as regression and
curriculum fixtures first.

## Levels 9–14 — Cold

- **9:** cold state acquired from cooling equipment; one obvious effect and one
  safe application.
- **10:** freeze water or condensate into a temporary traversal surface.
- **11:** cold stabilizes/locks moving factory machinery long enough to route.
- **12:** blast timing while a cold-created route expires.
- **13:** cold can create a recovery state as well as the primary route.
- **14:** cold + blast synthesis with an intentional mastery shortcut.

## Levels 15–19 — Heat

- **15:** heat introduction with blast still present; melt/thaw one unmistakable
  obstruction.
- **16:** heat reactivates a dormant charge or machine.
- **17:** choose when to preserve or spend a temporary heat state.
- **18:** cold and heat alter the same factory system in opposite readable ways.
- **19:** cold/heat/blast synthesis without requiring every mechanic at every
  step.

## Levels 20–25 — Magnetism and synthesis

- **20:** magnetism introduction; attach to suitable overhead infrastructure and
  release before discharge/drop.
- **21:** moving overhead carrier across a deep void or water hazard.
- **22:** blast launch into a magnetic catch window.
- **23:** selective state handoff; previous mechanics create setup or recovery.
- **24:** penultimate production/inspection challenge that tests route planning
  rather than raw precision.
- **25:** fair final synthesis leading directly into dispatch, with no arbitrary
  mechanic checklist.

## Ending beat

After Level 25, the scanner admits the robot to dispatch, reveals through
machinery/signage that its order was cancelled, and leaves an already-open exit.
The player keeps control. Crossing that threshold ends the game; waiting or
walking away from the scanner remains possible until then.

## Acceptance per level

Every authored level records primary route, launch jobs, landing and timing
windows, recovery, mastery shortcut, introduced/recombined skills, expected
first-clear duration, and evaluator results. A level is accepted only after the
real runtime is played; solver output alone is insufficient.
